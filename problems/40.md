# Problem 40 · The Tamagawa factor as a local term

**Kind.** Mathematical problem, research level. Thesis 9 ([State what is not established](../THESES.md#thesis-9)).

**Source.** *Fractured Adelic Geometry* [S04], Problem 22.7.1, p. 173; §2.8.1, p. 19; §56.3, p. 437. DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23585887](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23585887).

**Status in the source.** OPEN.

**Statement.** The source proposes three constructions of the bad-prime term $c_p$ of the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer: a vertical intersection multiplicity on the arithmetic surface, a Lefschetz–Verdier term of a fixed-point stack, and a determinant defect of the prismatic/Néron component lattice. Problem 22.7.1: “Construct the three local objects just described and prove that each has Euler characteristic c_p, compatibly with change of model, Frobenius action, and the integral orientation of the fundamental line.”

**What the source establishes.** The identification of $c_p$ as a Lefschetz–Verdier local term is listed among the book’s novelties, and the source fixes the test: “This is a particularly useful falsification test. Tate’s algorithm gives c_p independently for every database curve. If a proposed prismatic or fixed-point formula disagrees even once, that local construction is wrong. No appeal to BSD can repair such a disagreement.” The book lists this among the items that are “testable now, independently of BSD.”

**Proposed by.** [S04].

**Background.** [Tat75]; [BK90]; [BS22].

**Solved when.** The three constructions with proofs that each has Euler characteristic $c_p$ with the stated compatibilities; or a curve and a prime at which a proposed construction disagrees with Tate’s algorithm, which refutes that construction. The problem concerns local terms and does not bear on the truth of BSD.

**Literature check (28 October 2026).** No prismatic or fixed-point formula for the Tamagawa factor was found.

**Discussion.** [Issue 40](https://github.com/AlienMuse/grothendieckian-problems/issues/40). Solutions, partial results, counterexamples to the problem as stated and reports that it is already solved are all welcome; see [CONTRIBUTING](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
