# Problem 31 · How many ambiguity axes are there?

**Kind.** Mathematical problem, research level. Thesis 2 ([Status is part of the type](../THESES.md#thesis-2)).

**Source.** *GM-Omega-Infinity: Topos-Native Intelligence* [S02], Question 11.11.20, p. 2259. DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23538544](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23538544).

**Status in the source.** X.

**Statement.** The indeterminacy calculus of [S02, Ch. 11.9] separates the ways a reported number can depend on arbitrary choices. “Chapter 11.9 opened with three and closed with four: the comparison link of Definition 11.9.42 was found only after the first three had been written up, which is evidence that the enumeration is incomplete.” Determine whether routing tie-breaking is a fifth axis with useful exact invariants, and whether the enumeration is complete.

**What the source establishes.** “Whether tie rules form a separate typed transformation family with useful exact invariants remains open; no fifth calculus is asserted.” Simplicial orientation and numerical precision are examined and dismissed with reasons.

**Proposed by.** [S02].

**Background.** Gauge and presentation invariance [BBCV21]; [HG19].

**Solved when.** A typed transformation family for tie rules with its exact invariants, or a proof that tie rules reduce to the four existing axes; a completeness theorem for a stated class of instruments also solves the problem.

**Literature check (28 October 2026).** No result on this classification was found.

**Discussion.** [Issue 31](https://github.com/AlienMuse/grothendieckian-problems/issues/31). Solutions, partial results, counterexamples to the problem as stated and reports that it is already solved are all welcome; see [CONTRIBUTING](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
