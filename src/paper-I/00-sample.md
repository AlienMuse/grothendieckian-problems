# Introduction

*Layout sample. The text of this section is a placeholder; the paper's introduction is written in step S2.*

A reasoning system that works on mathematics has to decide what it stores, what it may assert, and who may promote an
assertion to a result. The seven works of the Grothendieckian Programme answer these questions with one discipline:
every artefact carries its world, its scope and its epistemic status, and no operation may enlarge what is reported
beyond what has been checked. This paper states that discipline as twelve theses.

# The theses

::: {.thesis #th-status title="Status is part of the type"}
::: claim
The epistemic status of a statement belongs to its type, not to a confidence score attached to it.
:::

::: {.field label="Argument"}
A confidence score orders statements on one scale; a status records what kind of support a statement has. The two are
different objects, and a system that collapses the second into the first loses the distinction between a checked
theorem, a definition, a research construction and an open obligation.
:::

::: {.field label="Evidence in the seven works"}
Series 01, Volume III, §1.3, p. 7974: "The series-wide codes E/S/D/R/C/O/Q are not confidence labels." The seven
codes are established (E), source-grounded synthesis (S), project-native definition (D), falsifiable research
construction (R), conjecture (C), obligation (O) and disputed (Q); "Validation state and epistemic status are
orthogonal".
:::
