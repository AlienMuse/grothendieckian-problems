Asked to consider a particular prime, Grothendieck is said to have answered "All right, take 57." Fifty-seven is
$3 \times 19$. The story is told against him, as an instance of a mathematician who did not think through examples; we
take it in the other direction as well. A list of problems drawn from first drafts will contain errors of its own, and
the place to report them is given with every problem below.

# Part I. The Machine {.unnumbered}

::: {.problem #p-01 title="The first rising-sea event"}
::: {.field label="Kind"}
Machine problem · programme level · Thesis 8.
:::

::: {.field label="Statement"}
Exhibit one operational rising-sea event in the sense of Series 01: a campaign that begins from an explicit
obstruction or language deficit, proposes a typed extension, compiles bounded probes, preserves or explicitly
surrenders recovery, records costs and residuals, and submits any status request to authority outside the producing
engines, and in which the original obstruction becomes tractable without the question being changed.
:::

::: {.field label="Source"}
Series 01, *GAI-MUA: The Grothendieckian Machine*, Volume III, §5.1, p. 8034 (status S/R), and §5.8, p. 8039.
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23480696.
:::

::: {.field label="What the source establishes"}
"Volume III does not turn that strategy into a universal theorem of problem solving. It turns it into a falsifiable
campaign record." The source fixes the sequence of the campaign and four ways in which an extension changes the
question instead of resolving it: it deletes the predicate expressing a contradiction, forgets the invariant detecting
failure, weakens equality until disagreement disappears, or renames an unknown as a new primitive.
:::

::: {.field label="Solved when"}
An independently replayable campaign record is published in which every step of the §5.1 sequence is present, none of
the four failures applies, and a reviewer outside the producing engines confirms that the original obstruction has
become tractable.
:::
